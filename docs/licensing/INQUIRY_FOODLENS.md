## Email body (ready to send)

> **Subject:** Request for dataset access and licensing terms — FoodLens (IMPROVE 2025)
>
> Dear Dr Hegde,
>
> I read your paper *"FoodLens: Fine-Grained and Multi-label Classification of
> Indian Food Images"* (IMPROVE 2025, Springer, DOI
> 10.1007/978-3-032-01169-5_16) with great interest. The 30,000-image,
> 140+-dish corpus you describe, with multi-label and fine-grained annotations
> mapped to a hierarchical Indian-food taxonomy, is considerably broader than any
> Indian food dataset we have been able to license.
>
> We are building **NutriCare**, a mobile application that recognises Indian
> dishes from photographs and estimates their nutritional content. We currently
> ship a 12-class model and are working toward a 100-class Indian-food
> classifier spanning North, South, East and West Indian cuisines. Your
> taxonomy and annotation scheme would be highly relevant.
>
> **We understand the dataset is not publicly available** — I have not found a
> public release, and I am not assuming the 30K figure implies public
> availability. I am writing to ask whether access might be possible, and on what
> terms.
>
> **1. Availability**
> - Has the dataset been released, or is a release planned?
> - If it is not open, is it available for external research use on request?
> - Is there a data access committee, internal review process, or licence
>   agreement we should apply through?
> - Are you able to share the **class list and per-class image counts** even if
>   the images themselves cannot be shared? That alone would help us align our
>   taxonomy with yours.
>
> **2. Licensing and commercial use**
> We must be explicit that this is a **commercial** product: the app is intended
> to be distributed commercially. So we would need to understand:
> - Whether any licence would permit **commercial** use, or whether access would
>   be limited to non-commercial research.
> - Whether a model **trained** on the images could be **distributed** as part of
>   a commercial application.
> - Whether the trained model could be **deployed as a hosted inference service**.
> - Whether **model weights** derived from the dataset could be released, and if
>   so under what conditions.
> - Any restriction on **derived datasets** (relabelled, re-cropped, or merged
>   with our own imagery).
> - Whether attribution or citation requirements apply, and in what form.
>
> **3. Provenance**
> - How were the 30,000 images sourced? The abstract notes that "more than 100k
>   photos are submitted each day on Google Maps on Indian restaurants"; I want
>   to be careful not to assume the training images are user-contributed Google

>   actual provenance and the rights basis for commercial licensing?
> - If the images are restaurant-menu or licensed-partnership content, does that
>   licence extend to third-party model training?
>
> **4. Alternatives**
> If full access is not possible, would you be willing to:
> - share the **taxonomy and label hierarchy** for us to use as a specification;
> - share **annotation guidelines**; or
> - point us toward a version of the data that is licensable?

**Cautions:**
- The abstract's mention of "more than 100k photos submitted each day on Google
  Maps" is context for *why* Indian food is under-served, not necessarily a
  statement that Maps user photos were used. The inquiry asks rather than
  assumes — if the images *are* Maps user content, licensing for commercial model
  training is very unlikely to be grantable by the authors.
- A 30K-image academic dataset is almost never released for unrestricted
  commercial use. Realistic outcomes, in order of likelihood: (a) no response,
  (b) research-only access, (c) taxonomy/guidelines only. Plan accordingly.
- Response from a `google.com` address may be slow; a follow-up after 2–3 weeks
  is reasonable.
